# P3 conclusion: selection remains blocked by reference data

## Decision

**Status: `blocked_by_reference_data`. No candidate selected, no model promoted, and no third-sensor decision made.** This is the outcome of the decision gate, not a claim that the independent validation/final experiment was performed.

The user authorized completing the feasible software work. That authorization does not create missing measurements and does not override the instruction to preserve model defaults. P4, P5 and the compatible parts of P6 therefore extend the existing experimental estimators without promoting a P2 candidate.

## Evidence inherited from the completed P2 experiment

The frozen four-setting experiment used 18 historical development IDs: 11 passed the original QA gates, seven remained excluded, and all 12 historical validation/test IDs were explicitly not candidate-scored. The 44 candidate/trial evaluations completed without errors. There were no independent verified dual-hub clock mappings, independent validation groups or locked final groups.

On the same supported optical subintervals of those 11 trials, unchanged legacy versus zero-delay ATE was 0.980574 versus 0.264658 m, heading RMSE 40.114177 versus 13.601630 degrees, and yaw-rate MAE 22.810759 versus 10.820088 degrees/s. These are conditional development measurements. The mean-speed opt-in did not change these real-data results because none of the eligible development trials activated the legacy one-wheel substitution.

Counterevidence is important: full-cache SL_01 ATE regressed from 3.592600 to 4.021050 m, and SL_02 still had 5.849680 m ATE and 8.142910 m endpoint error. Full-cache optical references include repaired samples. The single initial alignment of a supported subinterval can remove drift accumulated before that interval. The smaller subinterval error does not certify the complete maneuver.

The optional center-mean rule is physically correct in the tested ideal no-slip one-wheel case: 0 and 1 m/s wheel speeds imply a 0.5 m/s center speed and 0.26 m radius at 0.52 m track. It remains synthetically verified rather than athlete-data validated. Uncertain raw sensor scaling, mounting, clock transfer, dynamic gravity contamination and slip have not been uniquely separated.

## Experimental PyTorch residual v1 - 2026-09-08

A C3D-supervised residual BiGRU is now available for **explicit research review only**. It corrects signed speed and yaw rate on top of a zero-delay, center-mean dual-hub physics baseline, then integrates the rates. The checkpoint used 11 historical train trials; epoch 32 was selected from five validation trials only. Five historical `_05` trials were evaluated after selection but remain regression evidence rather than a pristine final holdout.

On the five validation trials and their longest raw-observation-supported C3D intervals, macro ATE was about **0.165 m** for PyTorch versus **0.284 m** for the zero-delay physics baseline and **0.574 m** for the frozen deployed recipe. Signed-speed MAE was about 0.0445 m/s and yaw-rate MAE about 5.55 deg/s. Heading RMSE (about 5.81 deg) was slightly worse than the zero-delay physics baseline (about 5.10 deg), and long/full-cache slalom drift remains a counterexample. These values justify continued investigation, not promotion.

The Windows source application can load the checkpoint explicitly without changing current_best. Numerical comparison of its runtime path against the research implementation over all 21 accepted corrected caches differs by at most `4.77e-7` in predicted rates and `5.96e-7 m` in XY, i.e. float32-level numerical parity. This does not turn the historical validation into independent evidence.

The 2026-09-05 Qualisys archive does not unblock P3: all 19 dynamic C3Ds contain no point trajectories (`POINT.USED=0`). Nineteen `L_WC_EST/R_WC_EST` pseudo C3Ds were generated from the IMU/model path only to guide visualization/manual QTM reconstruction. Because they are derived from the estimator inputs, training or scoring against them would be circular and is prohibited.

## What stays unchanged

Windows retains the bundled legacy classical XY+yaw recipe, including its scale and delay logic. Mobile retains the M4 XY-only ONNX asset. Neither the optional center-mean rule nor the zero-delay setting becomes an application default. Existing calibration, geometry, QA thresholds, raw recordings and all historical candidate reports are retained.

P2's default recipe SHA-256 is `8782781854f16468d0b1251e4414c50986b64405743cecdf9ad0f96ddcd477a5`. The final software-phase preservation audit checks the actual current files rather than relying only on this historical identifier.

## Evidence required to reopen this gate

1. Correct moving left/right wheel-marker optical exports, with verified marker-to-hub mapping, residual validity, units, sample rate, retained full maneuvers and independent reference-quality review.
2. A documented mapping from both IMU acquisition clocks to the reference clock, including rollover/reset behavior, pre/post clock observations and an independent shared event. Zeroing the beginning of both streams is not synchronization evidence.
3. Recorded sensor identity, actual flashed firmware/build, configured ranges and advertised conversion scales, plus physical mounting, effective wheel radii, track, load, surface and camber. Do not infer old range settings from the currently connected board.
4. Grouped calibration/development and validation captures, followed by a separately locked final day/athlete/remount group. Keep overlapping windows and repeated trials from the same group together. Historical July trial labels are not a new unseen holdout.
5. Acceptance thresholds agreed with the coach before examining final outcomes. The original plan's thresholds are discussion targets, not achieved performance or clinical standards.

Then select once on validation, freeze source/config/data identities, run the final set once, and report every failure and exclusion. A disappointing final result must remain in the report; it cannot trigger silent retuning on that same final set.

## Hardware conclusion

The present evidence does not justify adding a third IMU. A chassis sensor could be studied only after timing/range/mounting issues are controlled and a residual mechanism is identified that the added measurement can address. A third IMU is not an absolute position reference or a substitute for valid C3D data. No sensor purchase, firmware flash or hardware change was undertaken here.
